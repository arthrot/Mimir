# Durable AI-enabled business opportunities for two student programmers (2026)

_Research date: 2026-11-05. This is an opportunity map, not a promise of income. Prices, rules, duties, and regulations change; re-check them before committing capital._

## Executive conclusion

The best odds do **not** come from finding a secret Instagram course or copying a temporary “meta.” For two unusually capable programmers with little capital and limited business credibility, the strongest path is:

1. choose a narrow, expensive, document-heavy workflow;
2. sell a productized service that promises a verifiable business result;
3. do the ugly integration and operations work manually at first;
4. turn the repeated parts into vertical software and proprietary workflow data;
5. use one distribution channel that compounds: industry partners, an established software marketplace, or an authoritative data feed.

The most credible candidates are (1) compliance/workflow automation for a specific regulated niche, (2) procurement or pricing intelligence built on public data, and (3) marketplace software inside Shopify or Atlassian. A supplier-enabled business can work when it is a **B2B sourcing, QA, or compliance business**; undifferentiated direct-from-China dropshipping is substantially less attractive after the United States suspended duty-free de minimis treatment globally in 2025.[^cbp-deminimis]

“Make a million” must be defined:

- **$1M annual revenue** = $83,333 monthly revenue. That could be 10 service clients at about $8,333/month, 42 software customers at about $2,000/month, or 278 customers at $300/month.
- **$1M personal pre-tax income/profit** is far harder. At a hypothetical 50% operating margin, it requires $2M revenue; at a 25% net margin, $4M. E-commerce often has still thinner contribution margins after fulfillment, returns, payment fees, duties, and acquisition.

Reaching either in twelve months from zero is an aggressive outlier outcome. The useful objective is to build a machine that can plausibly reach $1M annual recurring revenue over 24–36 months, while using the first 90 days to prove that strangers will pay.

## How claims were evaluated

Instagram, Reddit, Discord, course pages, screenshots, and creator revenue claims are treated as **hypotheses**, not evidence. A method is promoted here only when at least one of the following exists:

- a mandatory workflow or regulatory change;
- a first-party platform with documented distribution and economics;
- authoritative public data that can be transformed into a decision product;
- a buyer with an identifiable budget and measurable return on investment.

All unit economics below are planning assumptions, not observed averages. Validate them with paid pilots.

## 1. Vertical workflow automation: the best starting vehicle

### What to build

Do not sell “AI automation.” Sell one completed outcome in one niche, for example:

- permit/application completeness checking and correction packets for specialty contractors;
- bid/no-bid qualification plus draft response assembly for small government contractors;
- customer-document intake, exception handling, and audit trails for a narrow insurer, clinic, freight forwarder, or equipment distributor;
- quote-to-order follow-up for a specific high-ticket trade, integrated with its existing CRM/accounting stack;
- supplier document, certificate, and specification validation for an importer in one product category.

The wedge should process documents, call existing systems, route exceptions to humans, and preserve a source-linked audit trail. A generic chatbot is not enough.

YC's current Requests for Startups explicitly identifies AI for city-hall workflows, government software, vertical models, and on-premise AI for regulated buyers as open opportunities; this is directional validation, not proof that a given idea has customers.[^yc-rfs] OpenAI's API pricing also shows why the model itself is rarely the economic bottleneck: model and tool usage can be metered, while the buyer can be charged for the workflow outcome.[^openai-pricing]

### Illustrative economics

Assume a client pays $7,500 setup and $3,000/month. If variable model, OCR, communications, and support cost is $600/month, monthly gross profit before founder labor is $2,400 (80%). Twenty-eight clients produce about $1.01M first-year revenue if all pay for 12 months plus setup; in reality, ramp time means more clients or larger contracts are needed.

The service-first version can charge more: 10 clients at $8,500/month equals $1.02M annual revenue, but delivery labor can easily cut operating margin below 50%. Software should emerge from repeated work, not precede sales.

### Barrier and risk

The moat is domain-specific exception data, integrations, and trust—not prompting. Avoid autonomous high-stakes decisions. Keep a human approval step, log sources, test on real historical cases, and contractually define the scope. Healthcare, credit, employment, legal, and government uses require specialist advice and stronger controls.

## 2. AI governance and evidence operations

### Why now

The EU AI Act is now progressively enforceable: AI literacy obligations have applied since February 2025; governance and general-purpose-model obligations since August 2025; transparency and enforcement powers began in August 2026; and high-risk-system requirements phase in later.[^eu-ai-act][^eu-enforcement] NIST publishes a voluntary AI Risk Management Framework and a generative-AI profile with concrete risk-management actions.[^nist-airmf]

That creates recurring work: inventorying AI systems, documenting vendors/data flows, mapping controls, recording evaluations and incidents, training staff, and producing evidence for customers or auditors.

### Product wedge

Start as an “AI evidence room” for one buyer profile—e.g., 50–500-person B2B SaaS companies selling into Europe. Ingest vendor contracts and architecture, generate a review queue, map evidence to NIST/EU controls, schedule reevaluations, and export customer-ready packets. Sell implementation plus a recurring evidence-maintenance subscription.

Illustrative target: 15 clients paying a $10,000 assessment and $5,000/month produces $1.05M in the first year if all are active for 12 months. A realistic ramp requires higher pricing, more clients, or more time.

### Barrier and risk

Never market the product as legal certification or guaranteed compliance. Partner with a lawyer, auditor, MSP, or virtual CISO and let them own legal conclusions. The moat is current evidence and integrations, not policy templates. Horizontal governance software is crowded, so narrow the buyer, regulation, and evidence workflow.

## 3. Government-contract intelligence and response operations

The GSA's public SAM.gov Opportunities API exposes current notices, set-asides, NAICS/PSC classifications, deadlines, attachments, and award information, with active data updated daily.[^sam-api] USAspending and agency data can be joined to past awards, while Census data can enrich geography and business context. The Census Bureau provides free APIs and keys.[^census-api]

### A useful product, not another alert feed

Build for one contractor segment, such as cybersecurity MSPs, facilities vendors, or scientific suppliers:

- rank solicitations against the firm's actual past performance and certifications;
- extract hard requirements and disqualifiers with citations;
- produce a compliance matrix and bid/no-bid memo;
- surface likely teaming partners and incumbent history;
- maintain a deadline/evidence room and human approval workflow.

Begin as a weekly analyst service at $1,500–$5,000/month. After 10–20 customers reveal repeated patterns, productize ingestion and qualification. At $2,000/month, 42 customers is roughly $1.0M ARR.

### Barrier and risk

Raw notices are free, so forwarding them has no moat. Accuracy matters; missing one mandatory clause destroys trust. Government sales and registrations can be slow, and bid-writing may create conflict, confidentiality, or procurement-integrity concerns. Keep source citations and do not promise awards.

## 4. Healthcare price-transparency data

US hospitals must publish machine-readable files containing gross, cash, payer-specific negotiated, and de-identified minimum/maximum charges.[^cms-hpt] CMS finalized additional 2026 requirements for actual dollar amounts and comparable data, with enforcement of revisions beginning in 2026.[^cms-2026]

This is a data-engineering opportunity, not a consumer price-comparison clone. Potential buyers include regional employers, benefits consultants, revenue-cycle firms, provider strategy teams, and specialty practices. A product can normalize files, detect contract anomalies, compare local rates, and generate negotiation evidence.

Ten customers at $100,000/year is $1M ARR, but this is the slowest and hardest option here. Files are large and inconsistent; clinical coding and contracting expertise are required; sales cycles can exceed six months. The durable asset is a clean longitudinal dataset plus buyer-specific decision models.

## 5. Ecosystem apps: Shopify and Atlassian

Distribution inside an existing work system is more credible than launching another standalone AI tool.

Shopify says developers keep 100% of their first $1M in lifetime gross app revenue (subject to a 2.9% processing fee), then 85% above the threshold.[^shopify-revshare] Atlassian reports more than 260,000 customers, 6,000+ apps, and $6B in lifetime Marketplace sales; qualifying Forge apps can receive 100% of gross revenue up to $1M lifetime Forge revenue, after which standard revenue share applies.[^atlassian-market][^atlassian-pricing]

### Better app wedges

- Shopify: landed-cost and supplier-document workflow; B2B quote/order exceptions; returns root-cause analysis tied to catalog edits; regulated product evidence; reconciliation across a specific marketplace/3PL.
- Atlassian: AI-change approval and evidence; regulated incident/runbook validation; customer-specific Jira workflow quality; migration or data-residency controls.

Avoid generic AI copywriters, review bots, summarizers, and chat-with-your-data apps. GitHub's own Marketplace already shows a dense field of AI code-review products, including free/open-source substitutes.[^github-code-review] A developer tool needs a narrow environment, proprietary telemetry, or a regulated workflow to survive.

At $99/month, an app needs about 842 continuously paying customers for $1M ARR; at $499/month, about 167. Marketplace discovery helps but is not automatic, and support/review requirements are real.

## 6. Supplier-enabled business: sell trust, not parcels

The durable “Chinese supplier method” is not copying a trending gadget. It is reducing the buyer's sourcing risk:

- supplier discovery plus identity/capability verification;
- sample coordination and independent inspection;
- specification translation and change control;
- landed-cost, tariff, and lead-time comparison;
- certificate/test-report collection for one product category;
- multi-supplier redundancy and defect/late-delivery scorecards.

Target small US/EU brands or industrial buyers that cannot justify a sourcing office. Charge a project fee, inspection coordination fee, and disclosed percentage of order value. A software layer can later manage RFQs, comparison, approvals, and evidence.

Alibaba's own terms illustrate why a supplier badge is not diligence. Trade Assurance protects only the quality and ship-date terms written into the online order and paid through Alibaba; its “Verified Supplier” program uses third-party assessment, but Alibaba disclaims guaranteeing the authenticity or accuracy of products and certificates.[^alibaba] The practical gate is: order samples; define a measurable golden sample, tolerances, packaging, and defect criteria in the purchase order; keep order and payment on-platform; commission independent pre-shipment inspection; run a small batch; and independently verify market-specific certificates.

The importer remains the compliance backstop. CBP says an importer of record remains responsible for correct documentation, duties, taxes, and fees even when it uses a customs broker.[^cbp-importer] For covered consumer products, CPSC requires importers to issue compliance certificates based on testing or a reasonable testing program, and electronic filing became mandatory for covered imports in July 2026.[^cpsc-cert] Avoid children's goods, ingestibles, cosmetics/medical claims, batteries/electronics, PPE, and other safety-critical categories as a first product.

### Why classic dropshipping is weaker

The US suspended duty-free de minimis treatment for low-value shipments from all countries effective August 29, 2025, so every model must include applicable duties, taxes, fees, and entry costs.[^cbp-deminimis] Shopify Basic currently starts at $29/month annually with card rates from 2.9% + $0.30; third-party transaction fees can apply.[^shopify-pricing] Amazon referral fees vary by category and can be 8–15% or more before fulfillment, storage, returns, ads, and inventory costs.[^amazon-fees]

Amazon permits third-party fulfillment only when the merchant remains seller of record, removes the other seller's identity from packing/invoices, and handles returns; buying from another retailer after the order and exposing that retailer is prohibited.[^amazon-dropship] TikTok Shop similarly treats retailer/marketplace-to-customer fulfillment as fraudulent; a seller may use a 3PL when it owns the inventory and remains responsible.[^tiktok-shipping] TikTok's current US referral fee is 6% on qualified orders, before seller-funded affiliate commissions and returns.[^tiktok-fees]

Illustrative $60 DTC order: $18 goods, $9 freight/duties, $6 fulfillment, $4 returns allowance, $2.04 payment fee, and $15 acquisition leaves $5.96 contribution (9.9%) before software, payroll, and overhead. At that contribution, $1M gross sales yields only about $99,000 before fixed costs. The numbers can be better, but a screenshot of revenue says nothing about profit or cash tied in stock.

If doing physical commerce, prefer boring replenishment items, replacement parts, or B2B kits with repeat demand and low return rates. Secure inspection rights, product-liability insurance, compliant labeling/testing, and preferably exclusive supply or a proprietary bundle.

## 7. What is mostly guru bait

| Claim | Reality check | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| “Faceless AI pages print money” | Attention is rented, content is easy to copy, monetization and reach depend on platforms, and synthetic personas create disclosure/reputation risk. | Audience experiment, not a durable core business |
| “AI LARP / fake founder / fake UGC” | Deception destroys trust. The FTC rule prohibits fake or false testimonials, purchasing fake reviews, and commercial use of fake followers/views.[^ftc-reviews] | Reject deceptive versions |
| “One-product dropshipping with no capital” | Duties, payment fees, ad spend, refunds, chargebacks, delivery times, and product liability remain; US de minimis is suspended. | Weak unless differentiated supply/brand |
| “Amazon automation/passive FBA” | Amazon documents category referral fees before FBA, storage, ads, and returns; inventory is active working capital.[^amazon-fees] | A retail operation, not passive income |
| “Done-for-you AI e-commerce store” | FTC cases allege buyers paid substantial initial and operating costs while many stores produced little or no sales; other operators were banned over unsupported passive-income and million-dollar-store claims.[^ftc-ecom] | Treat as a high-risk business opportunity, not an investment |
| “SMMA/AI agency in 7 days” | Service businesses can produce cash quickly, but generic delivery is replaceable and churn is high. | Viable only with a niche, outcome, and proof |
| “Scrape Instagram/LinkedIn and auto-DM everyone” | Platform enforcement, privacy, anti-spam, and account loss can erase the channel. US commercial email must satisfy CAN-SPAM requirements including truthful headers, ad disclosure, address, and opt-out.[^can-spam] | Do small-volume, relevant, compliant outreach |
| “Sell an AI-generated course” | Without demonstrated expertise or outcomes, production is trivial and distribution is the whole business. | Usually audience arbitrage |
| “Generic AI code reviewer” | A crowded GitHub category already includes established and free/open-source tools.[^github-code-review] | Only with a sharply differentiated wedge |
| “Buy reviews/followers to get traction” | The FTC explicitly prohibits buying/selling fake reviews and fake social indicators for commercial purposes.[^ftc-reviews] | Do not do it |

## Recommended 90-day execution

### Days 1–14: select by evidence

Pick three niches where the founders can reach 30 buyers through alumni, family, local businesses, professional associations, or targeted email. Conduct ten interviews per niche. Ask for the last real document, spreadsheet, error, delay, or rejected application—not opinions about AI. Score each workflow on annual cost, frequency, data accessibility, decision risk, buyer authority, and sales-cycle length.

Kill a niche unless at least three buyers share real artifacts and at least one agrees to a paid diagnostic or pilot. Do not build a platform.

### Days 15–30: sell a manually assisted outcome

Offer a fixed-scope pilot with baseline and success metric: hours saved, response time, error rate, qualified opportunities, recovered dollars, or cycle time. Price it at $1,000–$5,000—high enough to test demand. Use existing models and automation tools; preserve citations and a human approval step.

### Days 31–60: deliver and capture the schema

Deliver personally. Record every input, exception, approval, output, and integration. Turn those observations into a canonical data model, evaluation set, and runbook. Obtain permission for an anonymized case study. If clients will not renew, do not hide behind more features.

### Days 61–90: narrow, raise price, productize

Choose the niche with the highest willingness to pay and shortest path to repeatable delivery. Automate only the repeated bottleneck. Add audit logs, roles, billing, error monitoring, and export. Raise the price for the next cohort. Select one channel partner or marketplace rather than five social accounts.

### Weekly scoreboard

- buyer conversations;
- paid pilots offered/won;
- time-to-first-value;
- gross profit per delivery hour;
- renewal/expansion intent;
- number and severity of human corrections;
- share of output linked to authoritative sources;
- pipeline value weighted by actual stage, not followers or views.

## Months 4–12: milestone plan

### Months 4–6: prove repeatability

- Reach 5–10 paying customers in exactly one niche.
- Require at least 60% gross margin after variable labor and model/tool costs, or document a concrete automation path to it.
- Establish a narrow onboarding checklist, evaluation set, human-escalation policy, and security baseline.
- Win at least three renewals or paid expansions before broadening the product.
- Publish one evidence-backed case study and establish one channel partnership.

**Kill or pivot** if fewer than 2 of 10 qualified prospects accept a paid pilot, no customer renews, delivery requires reinventing the process for every account, or errors cannot be safely caught by a human workflow.

### Months 7–9: productize the repeated work

- Move repeated intake, evidence extraction, exception routing, audit logging, and report generation into software.
- Keep bespoke analysis as a premium service; do not automate judgment merely to claim SaaS margins.
- Aim for $20k–$40k MRR with positive contribution margin and no customer representing more than 25% of revenue.
- Add the integration or marketplace listing customers repeatedly request, not a generic feature backlog.

### Months 10–12: choose one growth engine

- If outbound is repeatable, add a domain-aware seller only after the founders can demonstrate the funnel.
- If partners originate deals, formalize referral/implementation economics and enablement.
- If a marketplace converts, invest in reviews, documentation, support, and a higher-priced enterprise tier.
- Budget from cash collected, not pipeline. Track churn, payback, founder delivery hours, and support burden weekly.

A defensible year-one outcome is a narrow product with strong renewals and a credible path to $1M ARR. Collecting $1M inside twelve months would remain an unusually fast result.

## How to vet a course or money-making program

The FTC Business Opportunity Rule and its buyer guidance provide the right posture: demand substantiation and time to investigate.[^ftc-bizop] Ask for the cohort median **net profit after every cost**, percentage of buyers profitable, refunds/chargebacks/account suspensions, ad spend, hours worked, and failed stores—not the best revenue screenshot. Speak to buyers selected by you. Walk away from countdown pressure, financing pressure, undisclosed upsells, or claims that platform rules, duties, and product liability do not matter.

## Decision recommendation

Start with **government-contract qualification/response operations** if the team can access contractors, or **AI governance evidence operations** if it can access B2B software firms. Both are low-capital, sellable as services before software, and supported by current regulatory or public-data infrastructure. Choose healthcare price data only with a domain partner. Treat Shopify/Atlassian apps as a distribution strategy after interviewing users in those ecosystems. Treat physical sourcing as a separate operational business, not a shortcut enabled by coding.

The critical unfair advantage for two student programmers is not raw implementation speed—AI has made that common. It is the ability to run rapid customer experiments, instrument the workflow, and compound proprietary exception data while competitors keep shipping generic wrappers.
